    Paste a payment CSV, upload one, or load the sample. Required columns: id, date, payee, type, amount, jurisdiction, description, vendor_age_days, payee_role. Optional columns for the fraud, laundering and corruption checks and the SatyaNetra layer: official, decision_days, method, payer, approver, account, linked_to_employee, bidders, deliverable, pep.

    Step 2 · Validate and clean

    Every row is checked before scoring. Rows with problems are rejected and shown here instead of being silently dropped.

    Step 3 · Baseline screening

    This is what a standard rules check sees: each payment on its own. Adjust the settings to see scores change instantly.

    Step 4 · SatyaNetra layer: links across records that single-payment rules miss

    Standard screening judges each payment alone, so wrongdoers keep each one small and spread them across parties. This layer links records: payments to the officials who benefit and the decisions that followed, duplicate payments, shared bank accounts, circular and pass-through flows, and approvers who keep favouring one payee.

    Where the risk sits in this file. All bars are calculated from the records in Step 1.

    Method and limitations
    SatyaNetra focuses on possible bribery and related corruption involving government officials, public-sector staff and company officials. Both offering/paying and demanding/accepting sides may be relevant. Every flag is a lead for human review, never a finding of guilt.
    
 
      
  
    • SatyaNetra screens payment data for indicators of bribery, fraud, money laundering and corruption. Flags are leads for human review, not legal findings.
      • 
  
    • The layer relies on fields such as official, approver, account and payer. Here they come from your CSV; extracting them from documents and source systems is a proposed IBM step, not built.
      • 
  
    • A high score means "worth a human review", not proof of wrongdoing.
      • 
  
    • Rule weights are illustrative team choices, not validated on real cases. No accuracy figure is claimed.
      • 
  
    • Red-flag types follow indicators commonly described in anti-bribery guidance such as the US DOJ/SEC FCPA resource guide. Sample jurisdictions J1 to J4 are fictional.
      • 
  
    • Data stays in your browser. No live feed or external API is used.

    Frequently asked questions
    
   
    
 
 
 
    16 questions
    
 
    Tip: press / to search FAQs. Answers distinguish current prototype features from future plans.
    

    
   
    1. What is SatyaNetra?
    SatyaNetra is a prototype concept for helping reviewers spot potential bribery, corruption, suspicious payments and conflicts of interest. It combines transaction-pattern checks with contextual links and human reports. A flag is a lead for review, not proof of wrongdoing.
    

    2. What are our data sources?
    Currently: the website runs on synthetic demonstration data entered or loaded in the browser. It is not connected to live government, company, banking or law-enforcement systems.
    Planned sources, subject to permission and legal review:
    • Organisation-provided accounts-payable/ERP ledgers, vendor masters and approval logs.
    • Procurement and tender portals, contract awards and change-order records, where permitted access exists.
    • Official company registry filings and public audit, court or sanction records where lawful and relevant.
    • Gift registers and declared conflicts of interest supplied by an authorised organisation.
    • Human reports and supporting documents, only after a secure reporting service is built.
    Every source would need source-by-source checks for access, accuracy, licensing, privacy and lawful use. No live source is connected today.
    

    3. How do we plan to build SatyaNetra?
    Our proposed roadmap is to validate the detection rules on synthetic data, build secure data ingestion and normalisation, connect authorised sources, add entity-linking and explainable risk signals, then pilot against appropriately approved historical cases. Reviewers should be able to inspect the evidence behind each flag. The website is currently a browser-based prototype, not a production service.
    

    4. What tools and technologies do we use?
    The current demo is implemented as an HTML, CSS and JavaScript website. Its sample-data parsing, rule-based scoring, visualisations and demo interactions run in the browser. The project proposes IBM Z/LinuxONE for secure, resilient transaction processing and IBM watsonx.ai for possible document-context extraction and case-summary assistance. Those IBM services are not connected in this prototype; the current assistant uses built-in rule-based responses, not a live AI model.
    

    5. How do we plan to run the service?
    The intended model is an organisation-authorised service with controlled access, encryption, role-based permissions, secure logs, defined retention periods, backup and incident-response procedures. Reports would be triaged by trained human reviewers and corroborated before escalation. These are design goals, not operational features of this standalone HTML demo. Today, the page does not send or securely store reports.
    

    6. Where will SatyaNetra be hosted or used?
    The deployment location has not been finalised. A future deployment could be hosted in an approved organisation-controlled environment or an appropriately assessed data centre, depending on the customer, data residency rules, security requirements and integration needs. The prototype currently runs locally in a web browser; it has no production server or connected backend.
    

    7. Which operating systems will it support?
    Because the prototype is a standard HTML/CSS/JavaScript website, it is intended to work in modern browsers on Windows, macOS and Linux, and potentially mobile browsers. Actual compatibility should be tested across current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari versions before release. A future hosted version would depend more on browser and network requirements than on a specific desktop OS. No formal compatibility certification has been completed.
    

    8. Can SatyaNetra detect bribery that leaves no digital trail?
    Not from transaction data alone. A cash demand with no record or witness may leave no detectable signal. A future secure human-report channel could capture an account of an offline demand, which reviewers could compare with available records. Missing or inaccurate data can also cause false positives or missed cases.
    

    9. How will it identify suspicious activity?
    The demo uses transparent, rule-based signals such as repeated payments near approval limits, duplicate records, vendor/official links, unusual timing and contextual patterns. A future version could add validated models and authorised data connections. Each flag should show why it was raised, and no risk score should be treated as a verdict.
    

    10. Can someone report anonymously?
    The current report form is a local demonstration only: it does not transmit or securely store a report and cannot guarantee anonymity. A live service would need careful privacy and safety design, including minimal data collection, secure storage, access controls, metadata handling, a retention policy and a clear explanation of anonymity limits. Until then, use an appropriate official reporting channel.
    

    11. What happens after a case is flagged or reported?
    The proposed workflow is human triage, evidence checks, a fair opportunity for relevant parties to respond, and escalation to an authorised compliance, audit or anti-corruption body when warranted. SatyaNetra is not intended to arrest, punish or publicly accuse anyone automatically.
    

    12. How will privacy and security be protected?
    Before production, the project would require a privacy impact assessment, a lawful basis for each data source, encryption in transit and at rest, least-privilege access, secure key management, retention/deletion rules, monitoring and incident response. These controls are proposed; this static prototype is not a secure complaint-handling platform. Do not enter real names or sensitive evidence into the demo.
    

    13. How will we test whether it works?
    First, use labelled synthetic examples to compare baseline rules with cross-record signals. Later, if approvals and privacy safeguards permit, evaluate on historical resolved cases and measure precision, recall, false positives and missed cases with reviewers. No real-world accuracy has been established yet.
    

    14. Is SatyaNetra already connected to IBM watsonx.ai or live data?
    No. IBM Z/LinuxONE and watsonx.ai are part of the proposed architecture, not connected services in this website. There are no live transaction feeds, government APIs, production AI model or secure reporting backend connected to the current demo.
    

    15. Who is SatyaNetra for?
    The intended users include compliance and internal audit teams, procurement and finance reviewers, public-sector oversight teams, and people who need a safe, lawful route to report a concern. Actual access and responsibilities would depend on the eventual deployment and its governing organisation.
    

    16. Is this legal advice or an official complaint portal?
    No. SatyaNetra is a prototype concept, not a government service, official complaint portal or substitute for legal advice. For a real complaint, verify the appropriate authority and its current process for your jurisdiction.
    For India, official resources include the Karnataka Lokayukta, Central Vigilance Commission and Lokpal of India. Their jurisdiction differs; check the official instructions before submitting information.
